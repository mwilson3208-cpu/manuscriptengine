## The money model

Power Reads earns from three streams, and the order matters: backend first, catalog royalties second, authority third. Build the system so every book feeds all three, and measure them separately so a book with modest royalties and strong lead flow is not mistaken for a failure.

| Stream | What it is | How a book drives it | What to measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Backend | EPN membership, coaching, Udemy and getepn.com courses, Boardroom Collective | Reader magnet inside the book, opt-in, nurture, offer | Opt-ins per 100 copies, leads per 100 opt-ins, revenue per 100 copies |
| Catalog royalties | KDP and IngramSpark compensation across every format | Series depth, format multiplication, backlist ads at break-even or better | Contribution after ads and reserves, per title and per series |
| Authority | Speaking, sponsorships, partnerships, TEDx-type platforms, client trust | Books as credentials and leave-behinds | Invitations and deals where the book was cited |

**Unit math on the verified v1 figures**

At $17.99 paperback and $7.99 ebook on Amazon, the v1 numbers hold: about $7.39 per paperback and $5.38 per ebook before ads. A title selling 40 paperbacks and 40 ebooks a month earns roughly $510 before ads, about $330 after a $180 ad test. That is a real but small business per title. It becomes a real business at catalog scale: 12 titles at that level is about $4,000 a month before ads, and backlist titles cost nothing to keep listed.

Now the backend. If a book converts 5 percent of readers to the email list and 3 percent of the list to a coaching or membership purchase, 100 copies produce 5 opt-ins and 0.15 buyers. At 1,000 copies a year that is 50 opt-ins and 1.5 buyers per title. One EPN annual membership or one coaching engagement at your current prices almost certainly exceeds the royalty on 100 copies. Fill in your real prices in Section 9; the point is that the funnel, not the royalty rate, is the lever worth the most work.

**What the educator research actually supports**

The v1 audit of 13 educators reduces to three transferable lessons. S.J. Scott showed that a themed nonfiction series feeding an email list is the durable model. Holmquist showed that a publisher who owns research and standards can scale with writers and editors. Penn showed that rights ownership and wide distribution protect the business. Everything else was fiction, low-content, or self-reported income that cannot be benchmarked. The Mikkelsen pattern (keyword-chasing AI volume) is the one Amazon is actively hostile to and the one to avoid.

## Two-lane catalog architecture

Run two lanes with different budgets, cycles, and jobs. The Authority Lane produces the books that carry your name and feed coaching. The Catalog Lane produces focused how-to titles that earn on search demand and build series depth. Both are full-content nonfiction. Neither is low-content.

|  | Authority Lane | Catalog Lane |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Job | Credential, trust asset, backend feeder | Royalty contribution and series depth |
| Length | 30 to 45k words | 15 to 25k words, complete and useful |
| Cycle | 60 to 90 days | 30 to 45 days |
| Source | Your frameworks, stories, client patterns | Your expertise on one narrow reader job |
| Author name | Michael Wilson | Michael Wilson, or a Power Reads house author if the niche is outside your personal brand |
| Budget | $1,200 to $2,500 with a designed cover and no outside editing | $500 to $1,200 with template interior and a designed cover |
| Validation bar | Reader job plus backend fit | Demand scorecard passes on comps and search |
| Formats | Ebook, paperback, hardcover, audiobook | Ebook, paperback, large print where the reader skews older |
| Launch | Full ethical launch with ARCs, EPN launch team, ads | Soft launch, series cross-link, capped ads |
| Target in year one | Two titles | Six to eight titles in two series |

**Catalog thesis**

Pick two niches where you have lived expertise and comps show steady sales, and go deep rather than wide. The obvious first series is business networking and social capital, extending Network Like A Millionaire into a three to five title set (weekly outreach system, networking for introverts, referral systems, networking for veterans entering business). The second candidate is owner-operator growth, extending The Profit Growth Engine and The Friday Scoreboard. AIO and The Shopping Cart Test are a third option if you want a marketing and ecommerce series, but keep Driply ownership disclosed and keep this plugin focused on books.

**House author decision**

A house author lets you publish Catalog Lane titles outside your personal brand without diluting it. If you use one, it must be a pen name, never a fabricated credentialed expert. Bios state that the title is published by Power Reads and written from the publisher's experience. Do not invent degrees, clients, or results for a pen name.

## The voice-first production engine

You write the book by talking it, chapter by chapter, against a locked outline. The transcript is the manuscript. AI cleans, tightens, and edits what you said through four scoped passes. You do one author read. Under the KDP definition that is AI-assisted: you created the content, AI edited and refined it. There is no outside editor and no human writer in this engine; the system does the editing and you are the only human gate.

The line that keeps it honest: every paragraph in the finished book must trace to something you said on the recording or typed yourself. If AI produces a passage with no source in the transcript, that passage is AI-generated. Either you rewrite it in your own words or the book is disclosed as AI-generated. There is no third option.

**Drafting, per chapter**

1. Lock the chapter brief: thesis, three to five beats, the story or client pattern, the exercise or template. One page. AI may draft the brief from your outline; you edit it.
2. Record the chapter. Talk through the brief for 25 to 45 minutes. Say the stories in full. Say the steps in order. Restate the point at the end. Explain it the way you would to a client.
3. Transcribe. Name the file TITLE_CH##_YYYYMMDD and store audio and transcript together.
4. Cleanup pass, logged: remove filler, fix grammar, order the beats as the brief lists them, mark every spot where the transcript is thin with MISSING. No new content.
5. You close the gaps. Each MISSING flag gets a short recording or typed paragraph from you. AI merges them. This is Draft 1.

**Editing, per chapter and then across the book**

Each pass may only make its own kind of edit. Each writes a change log with before and after for anything larger than a sentence, and any entry can be reverted by number.

| Pass | Scope | What it applies automatically | What it flags for you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Developmental | Argument, structure, reader outcome, missing steps, repetition between chapters | Cuts, moves, merges, reorders | Anything that needs new material becomes a MISSING flag. It never writes new passages |
| Line edit | Sentence clarity, rhythm, transitions, redundancy | Shorten, split, reorder. Tighten only, never rewrite in the model's own phrasing | Sentences it cannot fix without rewriting |
| Copyedit | Grammar, consistency, numbers, capitalization, term list, every claim against the claim ledger | All corrections | Claims the ledger cannot verify |
| Proofread | Typos, formatting, headings, front and back matter | All corrections | Layout questions |

**Two guards after every pass**

- Voice guard compares the edited chapter to the Master Voice Prompt and a 500-word sample of your unedited dictation, and reverts any edit that flattens you: longer sentences than you write, hedging you do not use, em dashes, generic coaching language.
- Fact guard compares edited against original and confirms every number, name, date, and claim survived.

**The author read**

After the proofread, you read the whole manuscript once with the change log beside you. Two to four hours per book. You revert what is not yours, record anything still MISSING, and freeze. After freeze the manuscript goes to layout; nothing is edited again except a proof correction.

A 30k-word Authority Lane book is roughly 12 chapters at 2,500 words, which is 12 recordings of about 35 minutes. That is seven to nine hours of talking plus gap closing and the author read. A 20k-word Catalog Lane title is about eight recordings.

**Where the time and money go now**

| Role | v1 | v2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Michael | 6 to 10 interviews, then review | 8 to 12 chapter recordings, gap closing, one author read per book, freeze authority |
| Writer | Drafts the full manuscript from transcripts | Removed |
| Editor | Developmental, copy, proof | Removed. The four passes and two guards do this work under the style sheet |
| Designer | Cover and interior | Cover and interior, platform template differences. The one outside role that stays |
| AI | Research, outline, critique | Brief drafting, cleanup, four editing passes, voice and fact guards, critique, metadata drafts, metrics summaries |

**Rules that do not move**

- A style sheet per book, locked before the first editing pass, holds the voice rules and the term list. Every pass reads it first.
- Keep the AI-use log per title: date, tool, pass, chapter, assisted or generated. Answer the KDP and IngramSpark disclosure questions from the log, not from memory.
- AI covers are disclosed as AI images. Human-designed covers are the default for Authority Lane.
- No client-confidential material in consumer AI tools without permission.
- Claim ledger before drafting: every statistic and case reference is Verified, Needs source, or Cut before the chapter is recorded, so you do not record claims you cannot keep.
- A chapter that fails the trace rule is not fixed by editing harder. It is re-recorded.
- No manuscript freezes without the author read. The engine edits; it does not publish.

## Demand validation that predicts sales

v1 was right that rank-to-dollar calculators are fiction, and wrong to throw out rank entirely. Used directionally, with thresholds, Best Sellers Rank is the only public sales proxy you have. v2 keeps the v1 scorecard and adds three checks: comps are actually selling, buyers pay the price you need, and readers search for the problem in words you can use.

**Scorecard (Catalog Lane must pass all six; Authority Lane may waive the first two with a written reason)**

| Check | Pass | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comps are selling | At least three comps hold a Kindle or paperback rank under 100,000 on three checks spread over two weeks | Amazon product pages, logged with dates. Snapshots on one day do not count |
| Price ceiling | At least two selling comps list the ebook at $5.99 or above and the paperback at $14.99 or above | If every comp sells only at $0.99 or $2.99, the niche cannot fund ads or an editor |
| Search demand | Amazon autocomplete returns the problem in at least five phrasings; a keyword tool shows steady, not spiking, interest | Record the phrases; they become subtitle and keyword language |
| Quality gap | Two concrete gaps you can fill from expertise, taken from 2 and 3 star reviews of comps | Missing templates, outdated advice, no system, no examples from real operators |
| Buyer language | Ten or more phrases from real readers or your clients | From reviews, client calls, EPN conversations |
| Ethical fit | The advice is inside your competence; no legal, tax, or medical overreach | Your call, written on the Problem Brief |

**Pilot conversations**

Ten to twenty short conversations with people in your network who match the reader, asked by you, not auto-messaged. Ask what they tried, what failed, what they would pay to fix it, and which book they almost bought. For Authority Lane titles, an existing client evidence packet can substitute if it is documented.

**Reader job sentence**

Every title starts with one sentence: a reader hires this book to ____ so they can ____. If the blank is a topic (leadership, AI, mindset) rather than a job (run a weekly outreach system that produces warm introductions), the title is not ready.

**Go, no-go, re-angle**

Five or six passes: go. Three or four: re-angle the promise and recheck within a week. Two or fewer: stop, and log why so the same idea does not come back in a different coat.

## Format and price ladder

A finished manuscript should earn from four to six listings, not two. Each added format is a new price point, a new search surface, and a new royalty line on work already paid for. v1 stopped at ebook and paperback. v2 adds hardcover, large print, and audiobook, and sets a default price ladder inside the verified royalty bands.

| Format | Default list (US) | Royalty basis | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ebook | $6.99 to $9.99 Authority, $4.99 to $7.99 Catalog | 70 percent of list minus delivery, inside the $2.99 to $12.99 band | Keep file size small; delivery is $0.15 per MB |
| Paperback | $16.99 to $19.99 Authority, $12.99 to $15.99 Catalog | 60 percent of list minus print cost at or above $9.99 | Owned ISBN, Expanded Distribution off when the title is also on IngramSpark |
| Hardcover | $24.99 to $29.99 Authority only | Same 50 or 60 percent structure minus a higher print cost; no Expanded Distribution on hardcover | Separate ISBN. Priced as the gift and credential edition |
| Large print | Paperback price plus $2 to $4 | Same paperback structure | Separate ISBN and separate listing. Worth it where readers skew 55 and up |
| Audiobook | $3.99 to $14.99 on Virtual Voice if invited | 40 percent on a la carte sales in the Virtual Voice beta; member listens paid by Audible allocation | Labeled as virtual voice. Invite-only. Your existing AI narration workflow covers non-Audible channels under each platform policy |
| IngramSpark print | Match KDP list | List minus trade discount minus print minus 1.875 percent | Start Non-returnable or Yes Destroy until a returns reserve is funded |

**Rules for the ladder**

- Price for contribution, not reach. An ebook at $2.99 earns about $1.80. The same book at $7.99 earns about $5.38. The lower price needs three sales to match one.
- Hardcover is an Authority Lane product. The buyer is the client, the speaking host, and the gift giver. Order it for your own leave-behinds at author cost.
- Audiobook decisions follow Select. If the ebook is in Select, read the current exclusivity terms before publishing audio anywhere else. Audible is moving all legacy ACX royalty contracts to its new model by the end of 2026, so confirm the live terms before relying on any audio number.
- Large print is a real reader service and a separate search result. It is also the cheapest additional listing you can make: same interior at 16 point type, same cover with a large print badge.

## Backlist compounding

The catalog earns when titles sell each other. A reader who finishes book one of a series and finds book two in the back matter, on the series page, and in the also-bought row is the cheapest sale you will ever make. v1 treated each title as a project. v2 treats the series as the product and the title as a unit.

**Series rules**

- Every Catalog Lane title belongs to a named series of three to five books before the first one is written. Plan the set, then write them in order of demand.
- Register the series on KDP so the series page exists and the books are linked on Amazon.
- Back matter in every book: a one-page preview of the next title, the full series list with links, the reader magnet, and a short author note. No review solicitation beyond a plain invitation.
- Front matter in every book: the series list. Readers who buy book three should find book one.
- Keep covers, subtitles, and interior layout consistent within a series so the set reads as one product on the shelf and in search.

**Bundles**

Once three titles in a series are live, publish a three-book ebook bundle at a price below the sum of the parts but above any single title, and a print omnibus if page count allows. The bundle is a new listing, a new price point, and a new ad target on a manuscript already written.

**Cross-promotion**

Authority Lane titles point readers to the Catalog Lane series that goes deeper on one chapter. Catalog Lane titles point readers up to the Authority Lane book and the EPN magnet. The ladder is deliberate: a $5.99 how-to book is the front door, the $17.99 flagship is the credential, and the backend is the destination.

**Backlist triage for the existing catalog**

Do this in the 7-day sprint. Sort every live title into one of three buckets and act on it.

| Bucket | Test | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep and grow | Sold in the last 90 days or has 10 or more reviews at 4.0 or above | Metadata pass, back matter refresh, add formats, add to a series, capped ad test |
| Fix | Good content, weak cover or description, no sales, few reviews | New cover and description first. Recheck after 60 days before spending on ads |
| Retire or fold | Thin, off-brand, or duplicative; no path to a series | Unpublish, or fold the content into a stronger title as a new edition. Dead listings dilute the author page |

A title with a KDP free ISBN that you want on IngramSpark needs a new edition and a new owned ISBN. Decide whether the title earns that before doing it.

## Backend funnel

Every book carries one reader magnet, and the magnet leads to one offer. The magnet is a tool the reader needs to apply the book: the scorecard, the outreach tracker, the weekly checklist. It is never the full ebook, which would conflict with Select. The opt-in lands on an EPN list, and the nurture sequence moves the reader toward one offer per book: the Udemy or getepn.com course for Catalog Lane titles, EPN membership or coaching for Authority Lane titles.

The funnel has five stages and one offer:

1. Reader buys the book
2. Reader opens the magnet link (8 to 15 percent of copies sold)
3. Reader opts in to the EPN list (50 to 70 percent of opens)
4. Five-email nurture teaches one more tool from the book
5. Reader buys the one offer for that book (2 to 5 percent of opt-ins within 90 days)

**Build once per book**

## Launch system and Amazon Ads structure

Your unfair advantage at launch is a community that already trusts you. EPN members, Power Table and Power Hour attendees, Books and Bourbon, your email list, and your YouTube audience are a launch team no keyword-chasing publisher has. Use them ethically and the first 30 days take care of the early reviews and the sales velocity that makes ads work. v1 had the ethics right and no launch plan. This is the plan.

**Authority Lane launch, 30 days**

1. Day minus 21: ARC batch to 30 to 50 people from the list, with the v1 ARC language: no obligation, honest opinion only. Log who received it.
2. Day minus 14: pre-order live for the ebook if the timeline allows; announce in EPN channels with the magnet, not a review ask.
3. Launch week: one email, three social posts, one short video, all approved by you. The ask is to buy and to tell one person who needs it. Reviews are invited plainly and never required.
4. Days 1 to 14: ad test runs under the caps below. Watch contribution, not rank.
5. Day 14 and Day 30: dashboard review and a decision: hold, scale, or stop ads.

**Catalog Lane launch**

Soft launch. Series cross-links go live the same day, the back matter of earlier series titles is updated to point at the new one, one email to the list, and a smaller ad test. The series does the selling.

**Select promotions, when the ebook is in Select**

A Kindle Countdown Deal or a free promotion in the second month can lift series read-through. Use free days only on book one of a series with books two and three already live; a free book with nothing behind it gives away the royalty and buys nothing.

**Amazon Ads structure, per title**

| Campaign | Targeting | Purpose | Starting budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Auto | Amazon chooses | Discover which terms and products convert | $5 a day |
| Reader language | 20 to 40 exact and phrase keywords from the validation scorecard | Capture the problem searches | $5 a day |
| Product targeting | The 10 to 20 comp titles from validation, by ASIN | Appear on the comps' pages | $5 a day |

Comp authors and titles are off limits in your metadata keywords. Targeting their product pages inside Amazon Ads is a standard, permitted feature of the ad platform; it is the one place comp names belong.

**Caps and gates, carried from v1 and tightened**

- $10 to $15 a day per title for 14 days, hard cap $300, approved by you before spend.
- Stop a campaign when ACOS holds above 1.5 times the simplified break-even (about 41 percent on the $17.99 paperback, about 67 percent on the $7.99 ebook) after enough clicks to judge, or when spend continues with no orders past the learning window.
- Scale by no more than 20 percent a week, and only on positive contribution.
- Move winning search terms from Auto into the Reader language campaign every two weeks. Move losers to negatives.
- Ads on a backlist title are only run after the fix bucket work is done. A new cover and description cost less than a month of ads on a listing that does not convert.

## Scorecard and kill criteria

A system that cannot tell you when to stop is not a system. v1 had a per-title dashboard and no verdict on the program. v2 keeps the dashboard, cuts it to the numbers that drive decisions, and adds a title-level and a program-level kill rule. Review on the Friday Scoreboard cadence: one hour, every week, same fields.

**Weekly title dashboard**

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Units by format | Which formats are earning; whether hardcover and large print pay for their setup |
| Royalties and compensation, all channels | The real top line |
| Ad spend and contribution after ads | The only ad number that matters |
| IngramSpark returns and reserve balance | Wholesale clawback exposure |
| Magnet opt-ins and offer buyers | The backend line, tagged by book |
| Reviews and average rating | Listing health and the earliest signal of a content problem |

**Title-level kill rules**

- An ad test with negative contribution after 14 days and no path to positive at 1.5 times break-even: stop ads. The title stays live; backlist costs nothing.
- A title with no sales in 90 days after the fix bucket work: move it to retire or fold.
- A Catalog Lane series whose first two titles both fail the 90-day test: stop the series. Do not write book three to rescue books one and two.

**Program-level kill rule**

At 12 months, with at least six Catalog Lane titles and one Authority Lane title live, the program continues only if at least one of these is true:

1. Catalog contribution after ads, reserves, and amortized production is positive for the last quarter.
2. The backend line shows at least three paying members or clients attributed to books in the last two quarters.
3. One series has a title in the keep and grow bucket that is still growing month over month.

If none is true, the model is not working for Power Reads as run, and the next decision is to change the lane, the niche, or the funnel, not to write another book the same way. Write that verdict down on the date it falls due so it cannot drift.

**Three capitals, carried from v1**

Financial is the contribution line above. Human is whether your designer delivers on schedule and whether your own recording and author-read hours are holding without crowding out EPN. Social is whether the books are producing introductions, invitations, and partners. Score all three on the same Friday review.
