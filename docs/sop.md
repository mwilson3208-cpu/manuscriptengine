Seven Stages from Idea to Earning Asset

## Revision note for Manuscript Engine

This file is the source document for the prompt templates in Manuscript Engine. It is the seven-stage production SOP as originally written, with four updates so it matches how Power Reads Publishing operates today:

1. The catalog publishes under Power Reads Publishing. The "Catalog Company" entity language from the original is retired.
2. Seven lanes, not five. Lane codes are listed in the Naming section.
3. No outside editors. Stage 4 is performed by the Manuscript Engine edit passes (developmental, line, copy, proof) with the operator as the final sign-off. The editorial steps are unchanged; the owner changed.
4. Course delivery, GoHighLevel, covers, Amazon Ads, and the 90-day Asset Review remain part of the business process but are outside the scope of Manuscript Engine. They are kept here so the SOP stays whole. Claude Code should encode Stages 1 through 5 (the manuscript and metadata work) and treat Stages 6 and 7 as context only.

## Purpose

This is the standard operating procedure for every catalog title published by Power Reads Publishing. It takes a raw idea through selection, spec, draft, edit, packaging, publishing, and launch, and hands a measured asset to the 90-Day Review. It exists so the pipeline runs the same way whether Michael is in the room or not.

**Scope.** Tier 2 niche nonfiction, Tier 3 structured content, and Tier 4 editions. Wilson titles follow the same stages with their own quality standard and their own GoHighLevel sub-account.

**Note on the course.** The course is built from the same edited manuscript at the same time as the cover and metadata, so it runs as a parallel track inside Stage 5. Seven stages, one pipeline, no separate queue.

## Naming, Folders, and Handoffs

**Title ID.** Every title gets an ID at Stage 1 and keeps it forever.

Format: LANE-TIER-SEQUENCE. Lane codes: TP (Thinksmith Puzzles), FA (Faith and Devotional), BZ (Business and Money), BH (Brain Health and Aging Well), PD (Personal Development), LD (Leadership and Purpose), VT (Veteran and Military Family). Example: BZ-2-014 is the fourteenth Tier 2 title in the Business lane. A Spanish edition adds ES: BZ-2-014-ES. A workbook companion is a new Tier 3 title with the parent ID recorded in its spec.

**Folder per title.** Catalog / Lane / Title ID / with subfolders 01 Spec, 02 Draft, 03 Edit, 04 Course, 05 Cover, 06 Interior, 07 Metadata, 08 Launch. In Manuscript Engine the equivalent is the book record, its versions, and its exports.

**Handoff rule.** A title moves to the next stage only when the exit gate checklist is complete. No skipped boxes. If a gate fails, the title goes back one stage with a note, and the failure is logged so we can see which gate is catching what.

## Stage 1: Select

**Purpose.** Find titles that can win before a single word is written. Most catalog failures are decided here.

**When.** Continuous. The idea bank is always full. Ten ideas scored per week, three approved.

**Inputs.** Lane keyword lists, the idea bank, last month's Asset Ledger (what is winning tells you what to make more of).

### Steps

1. Pull five candidate topics per lane from the idea bank, Amazon search suggestions, top-100 category lists, and reader questions in the lane's email replies.
2. For each candidate, search the primary keyword on Amazon and review the top 20 results.
3. Record for the top 20: how many titles have a sales rank under 100,000, the review count spread, price points, page counts, publish dates, and cover quality.
4. Look for the gap: outdated titles, weak covers, no workbook, no Spanish edition, no large print, no beginner entry, no title built for a specific reader situation.
5. Check that the topic can carry a three-lesson course with a real outcome. If the course would be filler, the book probably is too.
6. Score the idea on the Idea Scorecard.
7. Assign a Title ID to any idea that scores 20 or higher and add it to the approval list with the scorecard attached.

### Idea Scorecard (1 to 5 each, 30 possible)

- Demand: at least five of the top 20 under 100,000 sales rank
- Beatable competition: most of the top 20 under 200 reviews or with visibly weak covers and blurbs
- Clear gap: a specific angle none of the top 10 owns
- Margin: a paperback price that clears $3 net at the expected page count
- Lane fit: the pen name can credibly publish it and the lane list will want it
- Course fit: three lessons with a concrete outcome are obvious from the topic

Pass at 20. Anything under 15 is dropped. Between 15 and 19 goes back to the idea bank for a new angle.

**Output.** Title ID, completed scorecard, primary keyword, and a one-line angle.

**Exit gate.** Score of 20 or higher and Michael's approval logged.

**Common failure.** Scoring by enthusiasm. Fix: the scorecard is filled in before the idea is discussed, never after.

## Stage 2: Spec

**Purpose.** Turn an approved idea into a one-page brief that a writer, an editor, a designer, and a course builder can all execute from without asking questions.

**Inputs.** Title ID, scorecard, primary keyword, angle.

### Steps

1. Read the tables of contents of the top three comps.
2. Read ten one-star and ten three-star reviews of those comps. The complaints are the spec's angle and the course's outcome.
3. Write the specific reader: one person, one situation, one moment where they reach for this book.
4. Write the exact outcome the reader has by the last page.
5. List the examples, stories, or references the draft must use. Real, concrete, original.
6. Write the book promise in one sentence.
7. Write the unique angle versus the top 10 in one or two sentences.
8. Build the title and subtitle. Title carries the primary keyword or the outcome. Subtitle carries the reader and the promise.
9. Set seven KDP keywords and two categories.
10. Map the chapters. Tier 2: 8 to 12 chapters, each with a chapter promise, one example, and one action step. Set the word count target.
11. Write the course outcome in one sentence, three lesson titles, and the worksheet each lesson delivers.
12. Name the next book in the lane this title feeds, and note any planned editions if it wins.
13. Assign the pen name, tier, and lane voice card.

**Output.** Completed Title Spec Sheet.

**Exit gate, Gate 2.** Reader, outcome, and example are specific enough that two different writers would produce recognizably the same book. Promise is one sentence. Angle differs from the top 10. Course outcome is a result, not a topic. If any line reads generic, the spec goes back.

**Tier 3 note.** The spec adds format details: page count, prompt or entry structure, layout template, and the lane course it links to.

**Tier 4 note.** The spec is the parent's spec plus translator assignment, Spanish keywords and categories, and the Spanish course.

**Common failure.** A spec that describes a topic instead of a reader. Fix: if the reader line could apply to a thousand people, rewrite it until it applies to one.

## Stage 3: Draft

**Purpose.** Produce a complete, specific, action-driven manuscript from the spec. The draft is built chapter by chapter, never as one long generation.

**Inputs.** Title Spec Sheet, lane voice card.

### Steps

1. Load the lane voice card and the full spec.
2. Generate the expanded outline: for every chapter, the chapter promise, the key points, the specific example from the spec, the action step, and the transition to the next chapter. Review it against the spec and fix drift before drafting.
3. Draft chapters in order. Carry a short summary of the previous chapters forward so the book stays continuous and does not repeat itself.
4. Enforce the draft rules on every chapter: open with the reader's situation, no "in this chapter we will," one idea per chapter, at least one concrete example, one clear action step at the end, plain language at the lane's reading level.
5. Draft the front matter and back matter: title page, copyright page, the companion course page, a short introduction that states the promise, and the final page with the course offer and the next book.
6. Run a continuity and repetition pass across the assembled manuscript. Remove repeated points and repeated phrasing.
7. Run the AI-tell scan and the cliche scrub. Fix what they flag.
8. Flag every factual claim, statistic, or named reference in a separate list for verification.
9. Check word count against the spec. Under by more than 10 percent means a missing example or a thin chapter, not padding.

**Output.** Draft v1 plus the fact-check list.

**Exit gate.** Every chapter has an example and an action step. Word count within 10 percent of target. Fact-check list attached. AI-tell scan passed.

**Tier 3 note.** Drafting is layout and content generation from the format template. The same rules apply to any teaching text inside the workbook.

**Common failure.** Generating the whole book in one pass and getting a manuscript that says the same thing eight ways. Fix: one chapter at a time, summary carried forward, continuity pass at the end.

## Stage 4: Edit

**Purpose.** Every page is read, what does not serve the reader is cut, what is claimed is verified, and the book is signed off as delivering its promise. This is the account protection gate.

**Who.** Manuscript Engine edit passes in order: developmental, line, copy, proof. Michael reviews the change logs and signs Gate 3.

**Inputs.** Draft v1, fact-check list, Title Spec Sheet.

### Steps

1. Read the spec first, then the manuscript straight through once without marking.
2. Structural pass: does the book deliver the promise, in the order the reader needs it, with no missing step and no chapter that could be cut without loss.
3. Cut pass: remove filler, throat-clearing, restated points, and any paragraph the reader would skip. Target a 10 to 20 percent reduction.
4. Fact pass: verify every item on the fact-check list. Remove or replace anything that cannot be verified. Unverified claims are flagged for Michael, never silently kept.
5. Example pass: confirm every example is original, concrete, and specific to the reader in the spec. Examples are never rewritten by a pass; weak ones are flagged.
6. Line pass: sentence clarity, transitions, reading level, grammar, and any remaining AI patterns.
7. Action pass: every chapter ends with a clear action step, and the course offer placements are marked for Stage 5.
8. Produce a half-page note with the change log: promise delivered or not, biggest weakness, anything that needs a rewrite rather than a patch.
9. Michael reviews flagged items and the note, accepts or reverts changes, and runs a final read for typos and formatting artifacts.
10. Michael signs Gate 3.

**Output.** Manuscript Final, change logs, editor's note, signed Gate 3.

**Exit gate, Gate 3.** Promise delivered. Filler cut. Facts verified. Examples original. Every chapter ends in action. Editor's note has no open rewrite flags.

**Escalation.** If the note says the book needs a rewrite, the title goes back to Stage 3 with the note. It is not patched in Stage 4.

**Common failure.** Treating the edit as a proofread. Fix: the structural and cut passes run first. Proofreading is the last thing done, not the first.

## Stage 5: Package

**Purpose.** Turn the final manuscript into a sellable product and a working lead asset. Two tracks run in parallel from the same Manuscript Final.

**Inputs.** Manuscript Final, Title Spec Sheet, lane cover template, Catalog Lane Snapshot in GoHighLevel.

### Course track (outside Manuscript Engine)

1. Build the three lessons from the manuscript using the course-lesson-builder structure: objective, teaching, example, action step, recap. Each lesson is a 5 to 10 minute read.
2. Build one worksheet or checklist per lesson from the chapter action steps.
3. In the lane sub-account, duplicate the funnel from the template. Set the slug to the book's short URL, add the cover image and the outcome copy, and set the hidden Source Book ID field.
4. Create the membership product from the template, upload the three lessons and worksheets, and connect it to the delivery workflow.
5. Confirm the tags fire: book ID, lane, language, tier.
6. Generate the QR code for the short URL.
7. Insert the three placements into the manuscript: the front matter course page, the mid-book callout after the most actionable chapter, and the back matter offer page with the next book.
8. Test the full path on a phone: scan, load, opt in, receive the access email, log in, open lesson one, download the worksheet. Time the page load.

### Book track

1. Complete the cover brief from the spec: title, subtitle, pen name, lane palette, the one image or motif that signals the outcome.
2. Design the cover from the lane template. Produce ebook and paperback versions.
3. Thumbnail test: place the cover at Amazon search size next to the top five comps. If it disappears, redo it.
4. Write the blurb: one-line hook, the outcome, what is inside as short lines, who it is for, and the call to action. Lead with the outcome, never with the topic.
5. Confirm the seven keywords and two categories from the spec. Check that no keyword repeats a word already in the title.
6. Format the interior for ebook and paperback. Default trim 6 by 9. Check headings, front matter, the three course placements, and the back matter.
7. Export and open every file in the KDP previewer format. Fix anything that breaks.
8. Set pricing per protocol: ebook $2.99 to $5.99, paperback to clear $3 net.
9. Record the AI disclosure answer for this title in the metadata sheet and the ledger.
10. Build three A+ content modules from the lane template: the promise, what is inside, and the course offer.

**Output.** Live tested funnel and course, final manuscript with placements, cover files, interior files, metadata sheet, A+ files.

**Exit gate, Gate 4 (course and funnel).** Lessons match the spec. Worksheets deliver. Page loads on mobile in under three seconds. QR lands on the right page. Source Book field populates. Tags fire. Access email arrives within one minute. All three placements present in the interior.

**Exit gate, Gate 5 (package).** Cover survives the thumbnail test. Blurb leads with the outcome. Seven keywords and two categories set. AI disclosure recorded. Every file opens clean in the previewer. Pricing set.

**Hard rule.** The course goes live before the book is published, because the QR code is printed in the book. A funnel that fails its test holds the title. No exceptions.

**Common failure.** A beautiful cover that says nothing at thumbnail size. Fix: the thumbnail test is the only cover test that matters, because it is the only size the buyer sees first.

## Stage 6: Publish

**Purpose.** Get the title live on KDP with clean metadata, linked formats, and a ledger row, without ever touching the account's daily cap or its trust.

**When.** Never more than three new titles in a day.

### Steps

1. Log in to the one KDP account. Confirm the imprint is set correctly for the title.
2. Create the ebook first. Enter title, subtitle, pen name, description, keywords, and categories exactly from the metadata sheet. Answer the AI disclosure question exactly as recorded.
3. Upload the ebook interior and cover. Run the previewer once more. Set KDP Select enrollment for the launch period. Set the price.
4. Create the paperback linked to the ebook. Choose trim, paper, and bleed to match the export. Use the KDP free ISBN for Tier 2 and Tier 3 launches. Reserve owned ISBNs for winners going wide.
5. Upload the paperback interior and cover. Run the print previewer. Set the price to clear $3 net.
6. Add hardcover only if the spec calls for it and the lane supports the price.
7. Submit. Log the submission time and the Title ID.
8. Create the Asset Ledger row: Title ID, title, pen name, lane, tier, language, publish date, formats, production cost, course cost, course URL, AI disclosure answer.
9. When the title goes live, usually within 72 hours, verify the listing: formats linked, look inside working, price correct, categories showing. Claim the title on the pen name's Author Central page and add the A+ content.
10. Move the title to Stage 7.

**Exit gate.** Listing verified live, formats linked, ledger row complete, Author Central updated.

**Exceptions.** If KDP flags the title for content review, do not resubmit or edit while the review is open. Wait for the decision, then respond to exactly what was asked. If a file is rejected, fix only the rejection reason and resubmit once. Two rejections send the title back to Stage 5.

**Common failure.** Typing metadata from memory at the KDP screen. Fix: everything is copied from the metadata sheet. Nothing is typed fresh.

---

## Stage 7: Launch

**Purpose.** Give every title the same 90-day test so the verdicts are comparable, and hand a complete data row to the Asset Review.

### Steps

1. Day 0: send the launch broadcast to the lane list with the trackable link. Confirm the review request workflow is active for new opt-ins.
2. Day 0: create one Amazon auto campaign at $5 per day. Tier 3 titles run at $2 per day.
3. Day 7: send the second launch broadcast to the lane list.
4. Weekly, 15 minutes: check spend, ACOS, units, and the first opt-ins by Source Book. Pause nothing before day 30 unless spend runs with zero impressions.
5. Day 30: pull the search term report. Harvest the converting terms into a manual campaign. Lower the auto campaign to $2 per day.
6. Day 30 and day 60: update the ledger row with units, page reads, gross royalties, ad spend, net royalty, reviews, rating, best rank, opt-ins, opt-in rate, and next-book clicks.
7. Day 60: confirm total launch ad spend is at or under the $150 cap. If it is, hold. If not, cut to $2 per day.
8. Day 90: complete the ledger row with the month 2 and month 3 averages, compute Total Net, and put the title on the Asset Review agenda.
9. Asset Review: issue the verdict. Winner, Contender, Base, Parked, or Feeder. The title moves to its lifecycle stage and the next action is assigned.

**Exit gate.** Verdict issued and logged. Next action assigned.

**Common failure.** Turning off ads in week two because the title has not sold. Fix: nothing is judged before day 90. The rules exist so no one has to guess.

## Exceptions and Edge Cases

| Scenario | What to do |
|---|---|
| A title scored under 20 is wanted anyway | It goes into the idea bank for a new angle. It is not approved on preference. |
| Edit note flags a rewrite | Title returns to Stage 3 with the note. Never patched in Stage 4. |
| Funnel test fails | Title holds until the funnel passes. The book is never published ahead of its course. |
| KDP content review opened | Wait. Respond only to what is asked. No edits or resubmits during review. |
| File rejected twice | Title returns to Stage 5. |
| Daily cap reached | Remaining titles publish the next business day. Never stagger to a second account. |
| Translator unavailable | Tier 4 title waits. Machine translation is never published without native review. |
| Ad spend at cap before day 60 | Cut to $2 per day. Do not add budget before the verdict. |
| A lane produces no winners in three cohorts | Lane goes to the quarterly review before any new specs are approved in it. |
